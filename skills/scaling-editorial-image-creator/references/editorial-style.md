# Editorial image reference notes

Based on the user-provided Imagery Builder summary of Scout24 picture guidance (October 2026). The original agent draws on general imagery descriptions, Seeker campaign references and format guidelines. This file is a practical translation for article hero images, not an approved company-wide style manual.

## Keep these distinctions

- Housing direction: photorealistic, lived-in, observational, modestly imperfect European homes and neighborhoods. Lifestyle subjects use their relevant music, film, cultural or everyday context; an apartment or housing link is not mandatory.
- Seeker campaign reference: slightly stricter cool natural daylight; muted blue-green and gray-blue; realistic indoor shadows; natural human activity. Do not require the same subject, person, palette or poses in unrelated editorial stories.
- 24-35 mm and 35-50 mm are both described in source materials; neither is a universal lens requirement. Choose non-distorted detail, medium or contextual framing to match the picture's job.
- No verified corporate photo HEX values, exposure temperatures or universal dimensions exist in the provided summary. Do not invent them.
- Current SE compositions use 16:9 article title, News/category lead and grid images, plus 3:2 compact 120 × 80 px overview images. Keep the subject readable across these crops. The separate large/medium and small-list exports are specified in SKILL.md; the separate 80 × 56 px article reading list is outside this rollout. Check a retained older 16:7 crop only when it is actually requested.

## Motif possibilities by editorial angle

Use these as a repertoire, not a compulsory motif for each category. Apply the selection process in SKILL.md; prefer the concept that best communicates the specific article.

- Housing market / demographic change: generic everyday residential street or neighborhood with a mix of normal housing types, realistic transport or nearby services if visually natural. Never imply this is a photograph of a named city or claim visible vacancy/deterioration without evidence.
- Renovation / energy: ordinary residential building detail, window, insulation or heating device in credible installation context. For heating-cost comparisons, consider a recognizable radiator and thermostat; for checking consumption, consider a person reading a radiator-mounted heat-cost allocator. Use technically plausible objects without readable invented values, specifications or official labels.
- Moving / renting: candid everyday activity in a believable inhabited apartment, no product-placement poses or readable phone interfaces.
- Legal / policy / financial service: show the relevant housing context. For housing-benefit reform, an ordinary apartment building can establish the subject without inventing a recipient or suggesting residents are poor. For a concrete service action, show that action if it is visually meaningful.
- Building permits / completions: a plausible early construction stage, such as foundations and structural elements alongside occupied housing, can show the distance between a project and a finished home. Treat the site as generic, not a documented named project.
- Music / concert news: a generic performer from behind near a microphone, a non-identifiable stage silhouette or a relevant performance detail can provide a strong foreground subject. Use credible stage light and supporting atmosphere without inventing a specific venue, crowd size, celebrity likeness or documented performance.
- Film / television news: a camera, set detail or generic premiere situation may carry the story. Choose a meaningful subject instead of an unrelated glamorous face or a reproduction of a specific costume or scene.

## Composition for news cards

Start with the small card: readers should recognize one subject or action at 120 × 80 and 80 × 56 px. Prefer an expressive detail or medium view when it conveys the article. Use a wide architectural or spatial view when that context is itself meaningful and the main subject survives reduction. Do not apply arbitrary subject-percentage targets or promise higher click-through rates from close-ups.

Keep the background subordinate through viewpoint, separation, contrast and a limited number of supporting details. Natural depth is useful; extreme artificial blur is not a substitute for selecting a clear motif. Leave enough crop room for 16:7 without moving every subject far into the distance.

## Example: demographic change and housing values

Generate a photorealistic documentary-style editorial image for a German article explaining that population forecasts alone do not determine house values. Show an ordinary residential street in a generic small German town: a maintained modest house in foreground, a few different-age neighboring residential buildings, a road and an understated connection to the surrounding town. Everything plausible, everyday and unposed; the scene should not visually claim that the town is growing or shrinking. Eye-level medium-wide view, subtly asymmetric framing, neutral-to-cool soft daylight, restrained natural green and stone colors, believable shadows, slight signs of ordinary use, enough crop-safe room for a wide header. No spectacular mountain panorama, no postcard scenery, no forced before/after contrasts, no artificial decay, no people posing, no fabricated signs, text, numbers, logos, CGI or fantasy architecture. Generic illustrative setting, not a named district.

## Example: heat pump subsidy

Photorealistic editorial symbol image for a consumer article about the timing of a heat-pump subsidy application. Make a plausible external air-source heat pump the dominant subject, with enough of a normal house wall and credible connections to explain its installation context. Choose a close-to-medium view, realistic proportions, neutral daylight, natural textures and restrained garden context. Keep the device recognizable in a small teaser and a 16:7 crop. Informational rather than promotional; no readable labels, certificates, papers, graphs or stock-photo posing. This is not documentation of a real house or funded project.

## Example: named singer, generic concert symbol

For a short report about a named singer's ticket sales, frame an original generic singer from behind at a microphone in the foreground. Give the performer and microphone clear visual form; let stage equipment, credible concert light and an indistinct audience support the scene. Keep scale unspecified rather than inventing a stadium or a tiny local festival. Do not imitate the named singer's identifiable appearance, an existing performance or a press photograph. Use the specific-person/event symbolic caption required in SKILL.md. This is one concept option, not a compulsory motif for every music story.

## Editorial review questions

1. Does the visual match the article's actual angle and not an unrelated subplot?
2. Does the image feel more like a believable editorial photograph than a synthetic real-estate advertisement?
3. Are light, colors, people, architecture and object physics plausible?
4. Could it be mistaken for a factual photograph of a specific named property/place/event/person? If so, choose a clearly generic thematic motif first and include the visible symbolic-image clarification required in SKILL.md. Labeling alone does not fix a misleading scene.
5. Is it free of fake text, logos, extra UI, anatomy mistakes, or misleading details?
6. Does the dominant subject remain recognizable at small teaser size and survive the hero crop?
7. If recent motifs were supplied, is the selected subject, setting or framing meaningfully different?

## Editorial selection references

These support clarity, relevance and truthful visual framing; they are not Scout24 brand rules or evidence that generated imagery complies with news-agency photography standards.

- BBC Academy, *Features for the News website* (2019): reduce visual clutter and use strong form, color and light so a picture works at thumbnail size. https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/academy/academyfiles/Features_for_the_News_website_guide_v1-0.pdf
- Google Search Central, *Discover*: use relevant, compelling imagery and avoid misleading or exaggerated preview content. Technical delivery requirements belong to publishing, not a guarantee from the image generator. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover
- Nielsen Norman Group, *List Thumbnails on Mobile* (2015): small images must remain informative and recognizable; combine cropping with scaling rather than merely shrinking a detailed photograph. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-list-thumbnail/
- Mark E. Johnson, *Small Screen Impact in Photos* (2014): identify strong details and expressions and consider closer framing for small-screen photojournalism. This is editorial guidance, not a quantified click-through-rate claim. https://visualjournalism.info.uga.edu/2014/06/10/small-screen-impact-in-photos/
